The standby docs explain how to create and promote a standby cluster. They don't explain a **planned role swap** (primary ↔ standby) or what happens to the former primary. We measured both standby types on 3.1.0 (single runs, writers logging every acknowledged commit):

| Standby type | Planned swap result |
|---|---|
| Repo-only | 0 writes lost, 24.7 s downtime with manual waits. The **former primary diverged** (its shutdown checkpoint never reached the repo) and could not follow without a rebuild |
| Streaming + repo | 0 lost, 24.2 s. The former primary followed at once. Even an immediate promotion (1.9 s) self-healed through Patroni pg_rewind |

Please document:
1. A planned switchover procedure for each standby type, including the exact wait condition (standby replay LSN ≥ demoted primary's final LSN).
2. That repo-only standbys cannot receive the last partial WAL segment, so the former primary must be rebuilt (or a streaming source added) before it can follow.
3. RPO for repo-only DR: WAL reaches the repo at least every `archive_timeout` (60 s default). An unplanned site loss can lose up to that much, plus archive lag.
4. The `maxAcceptableLag` limitation for repo-only standbys (needs operator access to the primary) in the DR overview, not only on the lag page.
5. A warning that the operator does not prevent two primaries across clusters. Demote first and verify `pg_is_in_recovery() = t`.

Related operator requests are filed in percona/percona-postgresql-operator.
